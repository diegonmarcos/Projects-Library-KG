Eclipses: The Nodal Alignments

A New or Full Moon does not always cause an eclipse due to the Moon's 5° orbital tilt. An eclipse only occurs when the alignment happens exactly on the Lunar Nodes (where the orbital planes intersect).

The Venusian Rose Cycle

The 584-day (19.2 month) synodic alignment between Earth, Venus, and the Sun. As an interior planet, Venus never forms an opposition (180°) to the Sun; instead, it alternates between an Inferior Conjunction (between Earth and Sun) and a Superior Conjunction (behind the Sun), forming a 5-petaled pentagram over an 8-year span.

Inferior Conjunction ("New Venus")

Alignment: Sun — Venus — Earth • Retrograde Cazimi

Venus passes directly between Earth and the Sun during the peak of its retrograde cycle. Closest point to Earth. Transits from the Evening Star (Hesperus) to the Morning Star (Lucifer). Symbolic heart-reset and value purge.

Superior Conjunction ("Full Venus")

Alignment: Venus — Sun — Earth • Direct Motion Apex

Venus reaches the far side of the Sun, fully illuminated like a Full Moon, but obscured by solar glare. Transits from the Morning Star to the Evening Star. Represents peak social manifestation, externalized harmony, and relational realization.

The 8-Year Pentagram of Venus

Every 8 years, Earth and Venus complete 5 synodic cycles (5 × 584 = 2,920 days = 8 Earth years). Plotted geocentrically against the zodiac wheel, the 5 inferior conjunctions trace an exact, near-perfect 5-pointed geometric star known as the Rose of Venus.

Planetary Cycle Data

In astrology, a planet's "period" refers to how long it takes to travel through all 12 signs of the Zodiac from Earth's perspective. Because astrology is geocentric (meaning it looks at the sky from Earth), these astrological periods are heavily based on a planet's actual astronomical orbit, but viewed differently.

The Rulership Wheel

One sign in each half — the signs alternate masculine and feminine all the way around.

Classical / Traditional Rulers
Masculine (fire & air) Feminine (earth & water)

> The two lights sit at the top: the Sun rules Leo and the Moon rules Cancer. Reading outward from them, each of the five classical planets rules one sign on the solar (right) half and its mirror on the lunar (left) half — Mercury (Gemini/Virgo), Venus (Taurus/Libra), Mars (Aries/Scorpio), Jupiter (Pisces/Sagittarius) and Saturn (Aquarius/Capricorn) — which is why each planet's pair falls on one horizontal line across the wheel.
